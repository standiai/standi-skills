# Legal risk assessment

Evaluate and classify a legal or business risk using a structured severity-by-likelihood framework, then recommend an action. This is not legal advice — flag material risks for a qualified lawyer to review rather than deciding legal questions outright. The scoring thresholds below are a reasonable default; treat them as a starting point the organization can adjust to its own risk tolerance.

## Scoring a risk

Rate two dimensions on a 1-5 scale, then multiply them.

**Severity (impact if it happens):**
1. Negligible — minor inconvenience, no real financial/operational/reputational impact
2. Low — limited financial exposure (roughly under 1% of the relevant deal/contract value), minor disruption
3. Moderate — meaningful exposure (roughly 1-5% of value), noticeable disruption, possible limited public attention
4. High — substantial exposure (roughly 5-25% of value), significant disruption, likely public attention, possible regulatory interest
5. Critical — major exposure (over roughly 25% of value), fundamental disruption, real reputational damage, likely regulatory action

**Likelihood (probability it happens):**
1. Remote — would need exceptional circumstances, no precedent
2. Unlikely — possible but not expected, limited precedent
3. Possible — plausible, some precedent, foreseeable triggers
4. Likely — probably will happen, common in similar situations
5. Almost certain — expected, strong pattern or already in motion

**Risk score = severity × likelihood**

| Score | Level | Color |
|---|---|---|
| 1-4 | Low | Green |
| 5-9 | Medium | Yellow |
| 10-15 | High | Orange |
| 16-25 | Critical | Red |

## What to do at each level

**Green (1-4) — low risk**
Accept and note it in a risk log. Review periodically (quarterly or annually). No escalation needed — the responsible person can just track it.

**Yellow (5-9) — medium risk**
Put a specific mitigation or negotiation in place. Assign an owner. Review monthly or when a trigger event occurs. Brief the relevant business stakeholders. Define what would push this risk higher.

**Orange (10-15) — high risk**
Escalate to senior counsel or whoever leads legal for the organization. Build a specific mitigation plan and brief leadership. Review weekly or at defined milestones. Consider bringing in outside counsel. Document a full risk memo and a contingency plan for if it materializes.

**Red (16-25) — critical risk**
Escalate immediately to the most senior available leadership (general counsel, executive team, board as appropriate). Engage outside counsel right away. Stand up a response team with clear roles. Notify insurers if relevant. Preserve any evidence and consider a legal hold. Review daily until resolved.

## Writing up a risk assessment

```
Date:
Assessor:
Matter: [what's being assessed]
Confidential: [yes/no]

1. Risk description
2. Background/context
3. Severity: [1-5, label, why]
4. Likelihood: [1-5, label, why]
5. Risk score: [score, level]
6. What makes this worse (contributing factors)
7. What makes this better (mitigating factors)
8. Mitigation options (with effectiveness / cost/effort / recommended?)
9. Recommended approach
10. Residual risk after mitigation
11. Monitoring plan and re-check triggers
12. Next steps (action - owner - deadline)
```

For a running risk log, track per risk: ID, date identified, description, category (contract / regulatory / litigation / IP / data privacy / employment / corporate / other), severity, likelihood, score, level, owner, current mitigations, status (open / mitigated / accepted / closed), next review date.

## When to bring in outside counsel

**Always**, regardless of score:
- Active litigation (filed against or by the organization)
- Any government investigation or regulator inquiry
- Potential criminal exposure
- Anything touching securities filings/disclosures
- Anything requiring board notification or approval

**Strongly consider**:
- A genuinely novel legal question with no settled precedent
- Unfamiliar or conflicting jurisdictions
- Exposure beyond what the organization is comfortable carrying itself
- Specialized expertise not available in-house
- New regulation requiring a compliance program build-out
- M&A due diligence or deal structuring

**Worth considering**:
- A serious contract interpretation dispute with an important counterparty
- Employment claims (discrimination, harassment, wrongful termination, whistleblower)
- A possible data breach that might trigger notification duties
- An IP infringement claim, received or contemplated
- A coverage dispute with an insurer

When recommending outside counsel, weigh subject-matter fit, jurisdictional experience, industry familiarity, conflict clearance, and budget/fee structure.
