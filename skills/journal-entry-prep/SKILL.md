# Journal entry preparation

Prepare journal entries for month-end close with proper debits, credits, and supporting documentation. This assists with — but does not replace — a qualified financial professional's review; every entry should be checked before posting.

## Standard entry types

**Accounts payable accruals** — goods/services received but not yet invoiced.
Debit: expense (or capitalize if it qualifies as an asset). Credit: accrued liabilities.
Sources: open POs with confirmed receipt, contracts with unbilled work rendered, recurring vendor arrangements, unprocessed expense reports. Set these to auto-reverse next period, keep the estimation method consistent, and document the basis (PO amount, contract terms, historical run-rate).

**Fixed asset depreciation** — periodic expense for tangible/intangible assets.
Debit: depreciation/amortization expense. Credit: accumulated depreciation/amortization.
Methods: straight-line (cost minus salvage, divided by useful life — most common), declining balance (accelerated), units of production (based on actual usage). Run from the asset register; check new additions have correct useful life/method; check for disposals or impairments needing write-off.

**Prepaid expense amortization** — spreading a prepaid cost over its benefit period.
Debit: expense account. Credit: prepaid expense.
Common categories: insurance, software/subscriptions, prepaid rent, maintenance contracts, event deposits. Keep an amortization schedule with start/end dates and monthly amounts; watch for cancelled contracts needing accelerated write-off.

**Payroll accruals** — compensation and related costs for the period.
Salary: debit salary expense, credit accrued payroll. Bonus: debit bonus expense, credit accrued bonus. Benefits: debit benefits expense, credit accrued benefits. Payroll tax: debit payroll tax expense, credit accrued payroll taxes.
Base salary accrual on working days in the period vs. the pay period; make sure bonus accruals reflect actual plan terms; include employer-side taxes/benefits; track PTO liability if policy requires it.

**Revenue recognition** — recognizing revenue as performance obligations are met.
Recognizing previously deferred revenue: debit deferred revenue, credit revenue. New receivable: debit AR, credit revenue. Deferring revenue received in advance: debit cash/AR, credit deferred revenue.
Follow the standard five-step framework: identify the contract's distinct performance obligations, determine the transaction price, allocate it across obligations, recognize revenue as each obligation is satisfied. Keep contract-level detail for audit support.

## What every entry needs

1. A clear, specific memo describing what it records and why
2. How the amount was calculated (formula, schedule, source reference)
3. Source documents (PO/invoice numbers, contract references, payroll register)
4. The accounting period it applies to
5. Who prepared it and when
6. Evidence of review/approval per the approval matrix
7. Whether it auto-reverses, and when

## Approval matrix (adjust thresholds to your organization)

| Entry type | Amount | Approver |
|---|---|---|
| Standard recurring | any | Accounting manager |
| Non-recurring/manual | under $50K | Accounting manager |
| Non-recurring/manual | $50K-$250K | Controller |
| Non-recurring/manual | over $250K | CFO/VP Finance |
| Top-side/consolidation | any | Controller or above |
| Out-of-period adjustment | any | Controller or above |

## Review checklist before posting

- Debits equal credits
- Correct period, not a closed one
- Account codes exist and fit the transaction
- Amounts are accurate and match the supporting calculation
- Description is specific enough for an audit trail
- Department/cost-center/project coding is right
- Treatment matches prior periods and policy
- Auto-reversal is set correctly for accruals
- Supporting documentation is attached and referenced
- Amount is within the preparer's authority
- Not a duplicate of an existing entry
- Anything unusually large is explained

## Common errors to watch for

Unbalanced entries; posting to the wrong or closed period; debit/credit flipped; duplicate entries; wrong GL account (especially similar-looking codes); accrual missing its auto-reversal (causes double-counting); a recurring accrual left stale after circumstances changed; suspiciously round estimate amounts; wrong FX rate or date on a foreign-currency entry; missing intercompany elimination; something capitalized that should be expensed (or vice versa); a transaction booked in the wrong period relative to when it was delivered.
