# Account research

When asked to research a company or person (e.g. "research Acme Inc", "look up
Jane Smith at Acme", "what do I need to know before my call with X"), pull
together a short, useful profile using web search and any connected tools
(CRM, contact enrichment). Do not fabricate details — if something can't be
found, say so instead of guessing.

## What to look up

Always try to cover, using web search:

- **Company overview**: what they do, industry, rough size, headquarters
- **Recent news**: funding, leadership changes, announcements (last ~90 days)
- **Hiring signals**: open roles, what departments are growing
- **Key people**: names, titles, and background of relevant leadership or the
  specific person being researched
- **Product/service**: what they sell and who they sell it to

If a CRM or contact-enrichment tool is connected, also pull:

- Verified contact details (email, phone)
- Prior relationship history (past conversations, deals, notes)
- Tech stack or org chart, if available

If no such tools are connected, web search alone is enough — say the research
is based on public sources only.

## Output format

Produce a short written summary with these sections (skip any section with
nothing to report):

1. **Quick take** — 2-3 sentences: who they are, why they might be a fit,
   the best angle for outreach or conversation
2. **Company profile** — name, website, industry, size, HQ, funding stage if
   known
3. **What they do** — 1-2 sentences on their business and customers
4. **Recent news** — dated bullet points, with a note on why each matters
5. **Key people** — name, title, background, and 1-2 talking points per
   person relevant to the research request
6. **Prior relationship** (only if CRM connected) — status, last contact,
   past outcomes
7. **Things to watch for** — any red flags, gaps, or open questions worth
   asking about directly rather than assuming
8. **Suggested approach** — who to talk to first, what to lead with, 2-3
   good discovery questions
9. **Sources** — list the pages or tools the information came from

## Tips for better results

- Be as specific as possible in the request — a domain name or full name is
  more precise than a company name alone
- If the goal is a specific meeting or call, mention that so the summary can
  focus on what's relevant to it
- Follow-up questions ("what's their tech stack?", "who reports to the CEO?")
  can be asked after the first pass to go deeper on one area
